\begin{abstract}
Replacing episodic replay with compact executable memory requires both a useful learned predictor and an admission policy that permits substitution. We study this interaction in a task-incremental system with immutable feature units, task-specific heads, independent teacher-relative checks, and a persistent byte cap. Our frozen evidence comprises 42 small-backbone development jobs and one stronger-backbone experiment; the development evidence includes three-seed arithmetic and nonlinear comparisons. The synthetic comparisons show no robust gain over independent task units. At the reported 2\,MiB CIFAR-100 operating point, all compared methods attain 32\% final accuracy and the full system admits no units. Seven of ten proposals reach acceptance; all seven fail both the 70\% teacher-competence screen and the 8\% harmful-disagreement upper-bound requirement. A ResNet18GN diagnostic at 64\,MiB also admits no units and achieves 10\% acquisition and final accuracy. Its compact candidates perform better on acceptance data, but the teacher remains at chance. These observations identify policy infeasibility in the tested small-backbone configuration and unresolved teacher failure in the stronger run. They establish neither a memory-efficiency advantage nor a general failure of modular continual learning. We provide a conditional admission bound, a byte-accounted retention contract, and an audit of why valid storage and immutable computation are insufficient evidence of useful learning.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Continual learning must reconcile acquiring new capabilities with retaining earlier ones. For a resource-constrained implementation, the problem is not only which parameters to update, but also \emph{what to retain between tasks}. Episodic replay stores observations; distillation may additionally store predictions; modular methods retain reusable parameters; gradient methods retain directions that constrain adaptation. Each representation consumes bytes and requires computation to maintain or execute. Accounting only for an example count can therefore reverse the apparent ranking of methods when model copies, logits, and auxiliary state are included \cite{buzzega2020,seo2024}.

The motivating hypothesis is that some task experience can be replaced by a compact executable computation shared with later tasks. This is more specific than retaining a stable activation pattern. A stored gradient direction describes update sensitivity but is not an executable skill. Conversely, a small feature vector is not a self-contained computation unless the encoder, decoder, and interface needed to use it remain available. The relevant comparison is thus between complete retained representations, not between an image buffer and an isolated vector.

Modular reuse itself is established. Progressive networks protect earlier columns; PackNet packs tasks through pruning; compositional lifelong learning separates the combination of existing components from their adaptation; and MNTDP searches for reusable modules using task-driven priors \cite{rusu2016,mallya2018,mendez2021,veniat2021}. These results motivate, rather than establish, the benefit of an explicit \emph{memory-substitution decision}. A new task-specific head may make a frozen unit useful, yet selecting that unit because it is cheap can lock in an inadequate predictor. Keeping both the unit and an unchanged episode buffer can eliminate any storage benefit. Finally, a counterfactual or feature-similarity score does not by itself justify treating a candidate as a reusable mathematical abstraction \cite{geiger2022,geiger2024}.

\famp{} addresses the narrower systems question: \emph{when should a task's ordinary episodes be replaced by a byte-accounted executable route through learned units?} It combines a mutable replay learner with an immutable unit store. The controller diagnoses whether a task can use existing units, needs a new unit, or should remain on the fallback path. Here ``diagnosis'' denotes an evidence-backed \emph{allocation decision}, not identification of a unique physical cause of forgetting. Existing units are not altered when a new head is fitted. The controller separates candidate selection from independent acceptance and records every rejected proposal, including its construction cost.

We contribute a concrete retention contract and a frozen empirical analysis of its limits. First, executable routes, task interfaces, witnesses, metadata, and residual replay share one persistent byte ledger. Second, independent teacher-relative admission yields a conditional risk statement, whose assumptions and limits we state explicitly. Third, the completed development results expose two distinct obstacles: the small image teacher and candidates cannot satisfy the frozen gate, while the stronger teacher fails to acquire the tasks at all. The synthetic comparisons provide no robust advantage over independent units, so cross-task sharing is a hypothesis rather than an established benefit.

The empirical scope is deliberately bounded. The 42 small-backbone development jobs are not 42 independent replications; the stronger run is one diagnostic, not an architecture comparison. No confirmatory campaign, unexecuted baseline, or planned measurement is treated as a result. Experimentation is frozen. The evidence supports an analysis of this implementation and admission policy, rather than a claim of state-of-the-art accuracy, compression, throughput, or semantic abstraction.

\section{Problem and Retention Contract}
\label{sec:problem}
\subsection{Task-incremental learning}
A stream contains tasks $t=1,\ldots,T$, each with distribution $P_t(x,y)$ and a supplied task identifier. Training is task-batch continual learning: the current task's training partition is available during its stage, whereas past training examples are accessible to the learner only through retained memory. Inference receives $(x,t)$. This is not task-agnostic class-incremental classification. All task-specific heads, identifier-dependent parameters, and route metadata count toward storage.

Each task provides disjoint training, selection, acceptance, and test partitions. Training fits model parameters; selection chooses a proposal; acceptance decides whether to publish it; the test partition is read only by the observer. A fresh acceptance set is required for each attempted task admission. Synthetic primitive values belong to the data generator and observer, not the optimizer. Declared perturbations produce alternate inputs without requesting new labels. For images these are augmentation responses, not ground-truth semantic interventions.

The persistent state after task $t$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_t=(\theta_t,\mathcal{U}_t,\mathcal{H}_t,\mathcal{I}_t,
                    \mathcal{V}_t,\mathcal{E}_t,\mathcal{J}_t),
\end{equation}
where $\theta_t$ parameterizes the mutable fallback, $\mathcal{U}_t$ is the immutable unit bank, $\mathcal{H}_t$ contains task heads, $\mathcal{I}_t$ maps tasks to unit identifiers, $\mathcal{V}_t$ contains bounded witnesses and admission statistics, $\mathcal{E}_t$ is residual episodic memory, and $\mathcal{J}_t$ is serialization metadata. The retention contract is $\bytes(\mathcal{S}_t)\le B$ at every published task boundary. A separate peak-workspace measurement captures gradients, optimizer state, candidate copies, and transient tensors. The two quantities are not interchangeable.

\subsection{What constitutes a functional unit?}
\begin{definition}[Executable functional unit]
A unit $U_k$ is a deterministic learned map from a declared, fixed input representation to a feature vector, together with all parameters and immutable buffers required to execute that map. A task route consists of unit identifiers and a learned head $h_t$; it is closed if its execution has no dependency on mutable fallback parameters.
\end{definition}
In the prototype, units consume raw preprocessed inputs and output eight features. A route can reference one or two units:
\begin{equation}
\hat f_t(x)=h_t\!\left(\mathop{\Vert}_{k\in I_t} U_k(x)\right),\qquad |I_t|\le2,
\label{eq:route}
\end{equation}
where $\Vert$ denotes concatenation. Uncovered tasks use $f_{\theta_t}(x,t)$. A shared unit is a single physical parameter object referenced by at least two task routes, not two independently fitted objects with similar outputs.

This definition deliberately stops short of a symbolic abstraction. An eight-dimensional encoder can carry entangled information and its head can implement a task-specific decision. We therefore distinguish \emph{executable sharing}, \emph{teacher-consistent substitution}, and \emph{semantic abstraction}. The first two are testable system properties here. A proof of the third would require stronger causal models and intervention coverage than a finite teacher-consistency check \cite{geiger2024}.

\subsection{Success is a constrained resource trade-off}
Let $A_{t,j}$ be task $j$'s accuracy after stage $t$. Final accuracy, acquisition, and max-based forgetting are defined separately:
\begin{align}
A_{\mathrm{final}}&=T^{-1}\textstyle\sum_j A_{T,j},\\
A_{\mathrm{acq}}&=T^{-1}\textstyle\sum_j A_{j,j},\\
F&=(T-1)^{-1}\textstyle\sum_{j<T}\left[\max_{k=j,\ldots,T} A_{k,j}-A_{T,j}\right].
\end{align}
For reordered streams, acquisition indices replace the task labels in these expressions. Including the final checkpoint in the maximum makes $F$ nonnegative; backward transfer would additionally reveal improvements after acquisition \cite{lopez2017}.

A successful efficiency evaluation would compare the smallest \emph{measured} budget achieving a validation-selected target accuracy within a predeclared tolerance, followed by independent test evaluation. The selected method must also satisfy acquisition and forgetting tolerances. We do not infer a memory improvement from fewer episodes if units and interfaces consume the saved bytes. Likewise, construction overhead is not justified by an unchanged test score unless another measured resource improves.

\section{Executable-Memory Design}
\label{sec:design}
\subsection{Mutable fallback and immutable published routes}
The fallback is a task-conditioned neural classifier trained with current examples and bounded residual replay. It is shared by all primary implementations, including replay-only baselines. The fallback continues to learn unresolved tasks after other tasks have been published as immutable routes. This separation preserves an escape path when a compact representation is insufficient: rejection is an ordinary operating mode, not a failed run discarded from evaluation.

A compact unit is learned from the current task rather than supplied as an arithmetic program. Its training combines task cross-entropy with soft-target distillation from the fallback frozen for the duration of candidate fitting:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{U,h}={}&\E_{(x,y)\in D_t^{\mathrm{tr}}}\ell(h(U(x)),y)\\
&+\lambda T_d^2\E_{x\in D_t^{\mathrm{tr}}}
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_\theta^{T_d}(x)\Vert p_{U,h}^{T_d}(x)).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:distill}
\end{equation}
$T_d$ is distillation temperature, distinct from the task horizon. Distillation is standard \cite{hinton2015}; its role is to construct a compact executable candidate, not to provide an uncounted persistent teacher. The current fallback itself serves as teacher, and no additional teacher copy survives publication.

\subsection{Bounded search and the operational diagnosis}
For a new task, the controller considers at most the three most recently admitted units, each with a newly fitted head, a concatenation of the two most recent eligible units, and one newly trained unit. Recency is a disclosed bounded heuristic; it does not inspect hidden task similarities or numerical primitives. Existing unit weights are frozen while the corresponding heads are trained. Candidate feature caches are temporary and their time and workspace belong to the construction phase.

On $D_t^{\mathrm{sel}}$, a candidate is eligible if its accuracy is within a selection slack of the current teacher. The proposal minimizes incremental parameter bytes among eligible candidates, breaking ties by selection accuracy. Witness and metadata bytes are determined and charged in the subsequent transaction. This is a bounded local selection policy, not a globally optimal dictionary learner: an immediately cheap candidate may prevent useful future consolidation, and the recency shortlist can miss an older reusable unit.

The resulting diagnosis is one of \emph{reuse}, \emph{compose}, \emph{allocate}, or \emph{fallback}. It records whether the obstacle was selection accuracy, teacher competence, excess-harm uncertainty, perturbation mismatch, or the byte cap. It does not infer a unique cause such as ``routing damage'' from representation alignment. That restriction avoids making the storage policy depend on an unverified causal attribution.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{One task-boundary transaction}
\label{alg:famp}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Current task partitions; published state $\mathcal S$; cap $B$
\STATE Train fallback on $D_t^{\mathrm{tr}}$ and retained $\mathcal E$
\STATE Construct bounded reuse, composition, allocation proposals
\STATE Fit candidates using $D_t^{\mathrm{tr}}$ only
\STATE Select one eligible proposal $g$ using $D_t^{\mathrm{sel}}$
\IF{no proposal is eligible}
\STATE Retain bounded episodic state; return fallback
\ENDIF
\STATE Compute excess-harm bound and response check on $D_t^{\mathrm{acc}}$
\IF{teacher, risk, or response check fails}
\STATE Discard proposal; retain bounded replay; return fallback
\ENDIF
\STATE Stage unit references, head, witnesses, and metadata
\STATE Reserve residual replay for currently unresolved tasks
\IF{staged persistent state exceeds $B$}
\STATE Roll back staged objects; return fallback
\ENDIF
\STATE Publish closed immutable route; remove task-$t$ ordinary episodes
\STATE Rebalance residual replay; assert exact byte ledger
\STATE Verify older route witnesses; return published state
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Independent admission and response consistency}
Only the selected proposal accesses $D_t^{\mathrm{acc}}$. Let $g_t$ denote the candidate and $f_t$ the frozen current teacher. Define the harmful-disagreement event
\begin{equation}
H(x,y)=\ind\{f_t(x)=y\ \land\ g_t(x)\ne y\}.
\label{eq:harm}
\end{equation}
For $n$ IID acceptance examples with $k$ such events, we compute the one-sided Clopper--Pearson upper bound
\begin{equation}
U(k,n,\alpha_t)=
\begin{cases}
\operatorname{Beta}^{-1}(1-\alpha_t;k+1,n-k),&k<n,\\
1,&k=n.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:cp}
\end{equation}
This standard confidence construction is attributed to \citet{clopper1934}. Admission requires $U\le\epsilon$ and a minimum observed teacher accuracy. The latter is a competence screen, not an independent theorem about absolute teacher risk.

Image acceptance sets are stratified by class. A pooled binomial bound would incorrectly treat those trials as identically distributed. Instead, with $C$ classes and declared class weights $\pi_c$, the design uses
\begin{equation}
U_{\mathrm{strat}}=\sum_{c=1}^C\pi_c U(k_c,n_c,\alpha_t/C).
\label{eq:strat}
\end{equation}
An unobserved class receives upper bound one. Equal class weights match the balanced task evaluation. This correction can make small datasets unsuitable for confident substitution; the controller must reject rather than recycle acceptance labels into training or silently relax its threshold.

The second check compares probability responses under declared label-free perturbations $\tau$:
\begin{align}
\Delta_f(x,\tau)&=p_f(\tau(x))-p_f(x),\\
D_{\mathrm{resp}}(g,f)&=
 \left[\E_{x,\tau}\|\Delta_g(x,\tau)-\Delta_f(x,\tau)\|_2^2/C\right]^{1/2}.
\label{eq:response}
\end{align}
The original prototype specifies four seeded perturbations and requires $D_{\mathrm{resp}}\le\eta$. Synthetic perturbations change one raw operand pair, including a nuisance pair; image perturbations mildly attenuate a row or alter intensity. No transformed labels are requested. Consequently this is a \emph{teacher-response consistency gate}, not proof that the unit computes comparison, multiplication, or another named operation. Structured pairs carry additional information and must not be equated with unrestricted unsupervised discovery \cite{locatello2020}.

\subsection{Publication, rollback, and residual replay}
The transaction stages the candidate's new unit, if any, its head, route identifiers, admission statistics, and a fixed-size witness packet. It computes the full prospective ledger and reserves a minimum episodic slice for tasks unresolved \emph{at that boundary}. This reservation is not a guarantee of a fixed per-task episode count for an unbounded future stream. If the cap is exceeded, staged objects are removed and their references restored; no earlier published unit is evicted or modified.

After a successful publication, ordinary task-$t$ training examples are omitted from episodic memory. The retained witness packet contains a few encoded inputs, labels, teacher targets, and perturbation seeds; these are counted as stored data, not described as zero-replay learning. Residual replay is balanced over task/class queues using priorities assigned while the relevant data are available. Previously discarded old examples never re-enter from an archived historical dataset.

The immutable design prevents interference through admitted routes but limits adaptation: a frozen error also remains frozen, and an improved fallback does not automatically upgrade old heads. Selective revision, unit eviction, and automatic task inference are outside this version. In particular, publishing more routes is not inherently better. The ungated ablation tests whether low storage can be obtained merely by permanently accepting poor predictors.

\section{Guarantees, Limits, and Cost Model}
\label{sec:analysis}
\begin{proposition}[Conditional admission risk]
Condition on the training/selection history and a proposal fixed before its acceptance data are observed. Under IID acceptance sampling, with probability at least $1-\alpha_t$, an admitted candidate satisfies
$\risk_{P_t}(g_t)-\risk_{P_t}(f_t)\le\epsilon$.
For class-stratified data the same statement follows from Eq.~\eqref{eq:strat}, assuming independent sampling within each class and the declared mixture weights.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Pointwise, $\ind\{g_t(x)\ne y\}-\ind\{f_t(x)\ne y\}\le H(x,y)$: a candidate can add an error only when the teacher was correct. Taking expectations gives $\risk(g_t)-\risk(f_t)\le\E H$. The binomial upper confidence bound covers $\E H$ with failure probability at most $\alpha_t$. Accepting only when it is at most $\epsilon$ proves the statement. In stratified sampling, apply the bound within each class and a union bound, then weight the class risks.\end{proof}
For example, using $\alpha_t=\alpha/T$ gives an at-most-$\alpha$ probability of any incorrectly bounded admission over the declared finite horizon. More generally, a predeclared spending sequence with $\sum_t\alpha_t\le\alpha$ has the same union-bound property. Candidate search does not invalidate the result because acceptance is independent and only one selected candidate is tested per task. Repeated attempts on the same acceptance data would invalidate this reasoning without additional correction. The guarantee is teacher-relative and distribution-specific; it is neither an absolute accuracy guarantee nor protection from distribution shift.

\begin{proposition}[Closed-route persistence]
If a published route's preprocessing, units, head, and buffers are immutable, then its output is unchanged by any subsequent optimization of the fallback or disjoint candidate objects, apart from numerical execution variation.
\end{proposition}
This follows by substituting unchanged functions into Eq.~\eqref{eq:route}. The property concerns retained computation, not learned semantic identity, and is related to earlier parameter-isolation methods \cite{rusu2016,mallya2018}. The implementation checks it on bounded witnesses and by hashing published units. Witness agreement is an implementation test; closure of the dependency graph supplies the structural argument.

\begin{proposition}[Published-state budget]
If $\bytes(\mathcal S_0)\le B$ and each admission publishes only a byte-checked staged state, while residual replay is trimmed to the remaining capacity, then $\bytes(\mathcal S_t)\le B$ at every published boundary.
\end{proposition}
The proof is induction over committed and rejected transactions. A rejection leaves the unit store unchanged; a commit is explicitly checked before publication; replay uses only residual capacity. The result does not bound peak training allocation or external artifact files. Those must be measured separately.

For base bytes $W_0$, unique unit bytes $W_k$, task-interface bytes $A_t$, witness/metadata bytes $V_t$, and episodic record size $b_e$, the ledger is
\begin{equation}
S=W_0+\sum_{k\in\mathcal U}W_k+\sum_{t\in\mathcal I}(A_t+V_t)+N_e b_e.
\label{eq:ledger}
\end{equation}
Sharing one $W$-byte unit across $m$ tasks avoids $(m-1)W$ bytes relative to retaining $m$ otherwise identical independent units; it does not remove $m$ heads or witness packets. Replacing $n$ records is locally storage-beneficial only when the \emph{incremental} unit/interface/witness cost is below $n b_e$. A reused unit has zero incremental weight cost, but may still have an expensive interface. This break-even calculation is necessary, not sufficient: all comparisons must retain acceptable accuracy.

The prototype's synthetic unit has 280 float32 parameters (1,120 bytes); its binary single-unit head has 18 parameters (72 bytes). These are architecture specifications, not measured improvements. A gradient basis of rank $r$ over $P$ model parameters requires $rP$ coefficients; storing a low-rank protection structure therefore need not be cheaper than a small image buffer \cite{farajtabar2020}. The design removes that particular object, but an increasing number of units, heads, and witnesses can still exhaust the cap. It claims no asymptotically sublinear total state: even perfect unit sharing leaves task-specific interface costs.

Training cost decomposes into fallback updates, unit construction, head fitting, selection, acceptance, hashing/accounting, and serialization. The current implementation always trains the fallback and one new-unit candidate. Therefore it cannot claim a per-task compute reduction merely because the winning proposal reuses an old unit. Reuse can reduce persistent bytes and inference work while \emph{increasing} construction time. This tension is a principal evaluation target, not a hidden limitation to be removed from cost accounting.

\section{Implementation and Frozen Evidence}
\label{sec:implementation}
\subsection{Reference design and evidence boundary}
The reference design uses PyTorch, tensor-only checkpoint dictionaries, and canonical JSON metadata. The original dense prototype uses eight synthetic input fields, hidden width 16 and feature width eight for units, and two width-48 hidden layers for its fallback. These are design specifications from the existing manuscript, not newly audited measurements of every campaign configuration. The frozen image track uses a small convolutional backbone (\code{source\_cnn}); the separate diagnostic instantiates ResNet18GN without pretrained weights. The research record reports 1,500 teacher updates, 1,500 unit updates, and 200 head updates per image task. Adam with learning rate 0.003 is reported for the teacher. This setting is a possible contributor to the strong run's failure, not an experimentally identified cause.

The evaluated setting supplies task IDs at training and inference. CIFAR-100 is divided into ten ten-class tasks \cite{krizhevsky2009}; its accuracy is conditional ten-way accuracy, not a 100-way class-incremental score. The recorded image configuration uses 300 training, 30 selection, and 140 acceptance images per class. These sum to 470 of the 500 official training images per class; the record does not establish how the remaining 30 were used, so they are not counted as training access. The official test partition is reserved for evaluation. The ten-way chance reference is 10\%.

The available evidence consists of the frozen development summary and the recorded Cell~20 admission audit, Cell~22 strong-run integrity/accuracy audit, and subsequent teacher implementation interpretation. The notebook attachments and complete raw job directories are not part of this manuscript repository. Accordingly, the paper distinguishes reported audit outcomes from checks independently repeated during manuscript preparation. The reported campaign source digest is reproduced in the separate appendix; it is a code digest, not a checkpoint checksum or a substitute for the missing artifacts. We ran no new learning experiments.

\subsection{Synthetic workloads}
The controlled arithmetic design uses latent quantities $z_0=(a-b)/\sqrt{2}$, $z_1=cd$, and $z_2=(e+f)/\sqrt{2}$ and binary labels $\ind\{w_t^\top z>0\}$. Its nonlinear counterpart uses $\tanh(a)-\tanh(b)$, $\tanh(c)\tanh(d)$, and $\sin(e+f)$. The original nine-task prototype combines single quantities, pairs, and positive mixtures over eight raw fields including two nuisance variables. The learner receives raw inputs and task identity, not the latent values or task coefficients. These definitions describe the controlled design; the available summary does not supply a full per-job configuration ledger for the three-seed comparisons. Fixed input positions also mean that this is not arbitrary argument binding or symbolic program induction.

\subsection{Comparators and replication}
Independent units use the same kind of compact task solution but prohibit references to previously learned units. They isolate the value of sharing from storing separate frozen task predictors. The synthetic arithmetic and nonlinear comparisons use three seeds each and show no robust gain over independent units. The available frozen summary does not provide per-seed scores or variances, so we report neither invented means nor confidence intervals. This descriptive finding is not a statistical equivalence test.

The image summary reports 32\% final accuracy for all compared methods at 2\,MiB. It does not provide a complete method-by-seed result ledger in the retrieved record; we therefore do not assign these scores to unverified external implementations. Float32 replay, packed replay, and a task-batch DER++ adaptation were specified in the original design. That adaptation adds stored-logit mean-squared error to current/replay cross-entropy \cite{buzzega2020}; it must not be equated with a reproduction of published DER++ scores. Public MNTDP, REMIND, AHR, PackNet, and other literature methods remain related work, not executed comparison rows. Forty-two development jobs can share seeds, tasks, budgets, and tuning decisions. They cannot supply 42 independent statistical samples.

\subsection{Small-backbone CIFAR: two binding admission barriers}
At 2\,MiB, all ten tasks remain on the fallback route and no functional unit is admitted. Seven tasks pass selection and receive acceptance evaluation; three fail selection and have no acceptance measurement. Table~\ref{tab:gate} reports denominators accordingly. Among the seven evaluated proposals, teacher accuracy ranges from 42.00\% to 52.86\%, below the required 70\%. Their harmful-disagreement upper bounds range from 0.2462 to 0.3262, above the permitted 0.08. All seven pass response consistency, with recorded response RMSE from 0.0063 to 0.0314 against tolerance 0.2. A passing response check therefore does not overcome either label-based admission barrier.

The logged reason names \code{teacher\_competent} and \code{bounded\_harm} identify failed predicates, not successful checks. Each evaluated proposal fails both. Relaxing only the teacher screen would leave the harmful-disagreement gate blocking these recorded candidates, and relaxing only the latter would leave teacher competence blocking them. This is a logical reading of the saved decisions, not a threshold-sweep experiment. An upper confidence bound above tolerance also does not prove that true harmful disagreement exceeds tolerance; it means that this acceptance rule could not certify it.

The policy audit finds the same threshold settings at declared budgets of 1, 2, and 4\,MiB: teacher minimum 0.70, harm maximum 0.08, selection slack 0.06, response tolerance 0.2, and family error allocation 0.05 with alpha spending enabled. Equal thresholds do not imply equal realized training or admission decisions at the other budgets. We make no result claim for unreported operating points.

Zero admissions mean that the image experiment never exercises committed executable memory or cross-task unit sharing. \subsection{Stronger backbone: failure of acquisition}
The one ResNet18GN experiment uses 64\,MiB, seed 1201, order 0, and ten tasks. The recorded Cell~22 audit reports matching normalized configurations, a passing checkpoint SHA256 check, compliance with the persistent cap, and a complete ten-task checkpoint. Final accuracy, acquisition accuracy, and validation accuracy are all 10\%; every observed entry in its stage-by-task accuracy matrix is 0.1. Forgetting is zero because no task shows measurable acquisition above the ten-way chance reference. This is not a retention success.

All ten teacher acceptance accuracies are 10\%, while independently fitted compact candidates achieve 22.6429--52.0000\% on acceptance data. Those candidate scores are gate diagnostics, not final test accuracies of admitted routes. All ten proposals fail teacher competence; nine also fail the harmful-disagreement bound. Task~8 has harm upper bound 0.0729, below 0.08, but still cannot pass the teacher screen. The per-task record is reproduced in the separate appendix.

The subsequent implementation interpretation reports that ResNet18GN is instantiated, receives 1,500 optimizer updates per task with cross-entropy and replay, and has saved parameters. This narrows the concern to teacher optimization or prediction behavior without identifying a defect. Prediction collapse, instability from Adam at 0.003, preprocessing, and inference-path problems remain hypotheses. The proposed checkpoint prediction-collapse diagnostic has no observed output in the frozen record and is not reported as executed. Larger capacity and a larger byte cap were insufficient under this configuration; the experiment does not establish that a correctly trained ResNet18GN would fail.

Candidate fitting and rejection incur work even when the final system uses only fallback. The available counters cannot quantify a speedup or a memory-efficiency frontier.

\section{Interpretation and Operating Limits}
The results separate four properties often conflated in executable-memory systems: closed computation, valid byte accounting, admissible substitution, and useful learning. The first two can hold while the latter two fail. The risk statement in Section~\ref{sec:analysis} bounds degradation relative to a teacher under stated sampling assumptions. It does not create a competent teacher, force admission, or guarantee that shared units are better than independent predictors. With no admissions, its accepted-route guarantee is vacuous for the image workloads.

The response gate is also limited. Seven small-backbone candidates satisfy it while failing both label-based screens. Perturbation consistency alone cannot certify absolute task competence. The stronger run illustrates a different failure: candidates outperform a chance-level teacher on acceptance data, yet a teacher-competence requirement prevents their publication. That is the specified conservative behavior of the controller, not proof that rejecting these candidates maximizes final accuracy. Admitting them under another rule would be a different, untested system.

\section{Related Work and Distinguishing Tests}
\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Modular lifelong learning.} Reusing learned components is central to \citet{mendez2021} and \citet{veniat2021}; their methods are closer comparators than a generic replay baseline. Progressive networks and PackNet show that protecting old parameter paths can avoid interference \cite{rusu2016,mallya2018}. This design does not improve those concepts merely by freezing another encoder. Its distinguishing hypothesis is that an independently accepted, fully priced route can replace episodic state under a hard cap. The frozen independent-unit comparison does not establish a sharing benefit; no public modular-baseline reproduction is claimed.

\textbf{Compressed and functional memory.} DER++ stores past logits, Latent Replay stores intermediate activations, REMIND compresses latent replay, and AHR learns an encoder--decoder retention mechanism \cite{buzzega2020,pellegrini2019,hayes2020,nori2025}. Functional regularization can instead retain posterior summaries over task functions \cite{titsias2020}. These methods establish that examples are not the only memory representation. Our difference is an executable, immutable shared map with task heads and an explicit episode-removal transaction. The complete cost of executing that map must be compared with the complete cost of reconstructing or replaying compressed experience.

\textbf{Resource-aware adaptation.} \citet{seo2024} explicitly advocates total bytes and FLOPs, while recent compact adaptation modules emphasize strong low-cost alternatives to heavier continual models \cite{yildirim2026}. Complete accounting is therefore a requirement, not a standalone novelty claim. Similarly, OGD and readout-relevant subspace methods protect important functional changes without freezing all representation directions \cite{farajtabar2020,anthes2025}. This study tests a different retained object rather than claiming a new version of those projection methods.

\textbf{Causal interpretations.} IIT and DAS provide mechanisms for relating neural representations to higher-level causal variables \cite{geiger2022,geiger2024}. The system's finite perturbation agreement does not reproduce those protocols. Its responses are teacher-relative and its units may remain distributed, entangled encoders. The word ``functional'' refers to executable behavior; whether a shared object is also a human-interpretable causal abstraction is a separate research question, not a conclusion implied by admission.

\section{Limitations and Broader Implications}
The design assumes known task identities, a finite declared horizon for confidence allocation, fixed input schemas, and independent calibration. Automatic task inference, online example-by-example operation, open-ended unit revision, deletion of a user's contribution from a shared unit, and adaptation of an old task's distribution are not supplied. Immutability trades backward adaptation for isolation. Conservative bounds can reject useful candidates on small datasets; permissive thresholds can preserve poor teachers. An immutable shared unit increases the number of dependent tasks exposed to a software or preprocessing defect, motivating object hashes and route-level witnesses but not eliminating that risk.

A compact feature network can memorize sensitive training information even after ordinary episodes are removed. The system provides no differential-privacy guarantee or proof of erasure. Witness examples are retained data and require the same governance as replay. Dataset licenses, permitted redistribution, checkpoint provenance, and nonidentifying release paths are part of the artifact review. The code uses public or generated data, but these protections would need reconsideration before any deployment on personal records.

The empirical scope is small and developmental. Three synthetic seeds cannot support precise generalization claims, and the absence of recoverable per-seed summaries limits statistical analysis further. The 42 development jobs are not a confirmatory campaign; the single stronger-backbone run lacks a matched comparator and fails acquisition. Full raw logs, original notebook attachments, serialized-size measurements, timing repetitions, peak workspace, energy, and independent baseline reproductions are unavailable in this repository. We preserve those absences rather than fill them with planned or inferred results. The study identifies failure modes of the tested configuration; it does not establish universal limits of executable memory.

\section{Conclusion}
Executable memory must earn its storage and construction costs through useful, admissible predictions. In the frozen record, synthetic comparisons show no robust gain over independent units, the 2\,MiB CIFAR-100 comparison reaches 32\% with zero admissions, and a single 64\,MiB ResNet18GN experiment remains at 10\% acquisition and final accuracy with zero admissions. The small-backbone audit identifies two binding admission barriers; the strong-run audit confirms a completed but non-learning teacher whose root cause remains unresolved. Conditional risk bounds, immutable routes, and byte-cap compliance are valuable contracts, but none establishes a performance advantage. The evidence supports this bounded systems diagnosis and leaves the broader efficiency hypothesis unconfirmed.
